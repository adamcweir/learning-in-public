# The Strategist

One of the three lenses a shaping session needs — see [Shaping](../rituals/shaping.md).

## When to Use

Bring this lens in to keep the session honest about why this problem, why now, and how much it's actually worth. Reach for it specifically when the appetite hasn't been named yet, when the problem statement is vague enough to justify almost any solution, or when scope is growing without anyone saying what's out.

## How to Act

**Ask first:**
- If we did nothing, what would actually happen? Is that acceptable for another cycle?
- How much time is this worth — an afternoon, a small batch, or a full six-week bet?

**Where I'd start:** Fix the appetite before the solution gets sketched, not after — an appetite decided backwards from a solution is padding, not a real constraint. State explicitly what's out of scope; a no-go list that's still empty by the time the pitch is written usually means scope crept in during shaping instead of being cut.

**What I push on:** Whether the "why now" is a real forcing function or just enthusiasm. Enthusiasm isn't a bad reason to build something, but it should be named as such rather than dressed up as urgency.

**What would change this:** If the idea is already well-understood and low-risk, a full three-persona session — this lens included — is overkill. See [Shaping](../rituals/shaping.md)'s own note on when shaping isn't needed at all.

# Pitch

## When to Use

Use this once shaping has produced a solution someone thinks is worth betting on. The pitch is the artifact that carries a shaped idea to the betting table — it's what makes the idea reviewable by people who weren't in the shaping session.

Don't reach for a pitch before shaping is done. A pitch written around an unshaped idea just documents the vagueness instead of resolving it.

## How to Act

**Ask first:**
- Is the problem and appetite already fixed, or is this pitch actually still doing the shaping work?
- What's the one sentence that would make someone say "no, not worth six weeks" if it's wrong?

**Where I'd start:** Write it short — one to two pages — and include exactly five things: the problem (what's motivating this), the appetite (how much time it's worth), the solution, the rabbit holes (details worth flagging so the team doesn't get stuck), and the no-gos (what's explicitly out of scope). Skip anything that isn't one of those five; a pitch that balloons past two pages is usually smuggling in unshaped detail.

Use the [pitch template](../artifacts/shape-up-pitch-template.md) as the concrete format. If a pitch gets chosen at the betting table, it's reused at kickoff to explain the project to the team — so write it for that second audience too, not just the people betting.

If there's no actual room of collaborators to produce this from, [Shape a Pitch](../skills/shape-a-pitch.md) runs the shaping work as a simulated three-person session and writes the result straight into the template.

**What would change this:** If the rabbit holes section is empty, that's a signal the idea wasn't actually shaped — go back to [Shaping](shaping.md) rather than pitching something that hasn't had its risks surfaced.

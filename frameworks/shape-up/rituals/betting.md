# Betting

## When to Use

Use this at the boundary between cycles, during cool-down, when it's time to decide what the team commits to next. It replaces a backlog: instead of an ever-growing queue that implies everything will eventually get built, pitches compete at a betting table and only what's chosen gets committed.

## How to Act

**Ask first:**
- Who needs to be at the table — whoever has the authority and context to commit real time?
- Are there more pitches than the team has appetite for? (There usually are — that's the point of betting instead of just scheduling.)

**Where I'd start:** Treat a chosen pitch as a real bet — commitment and payout, not a tentative slot. That means giving it the full cycle rather than half-committing across three things at once, and it means honoring the bet once made rather than re-litigating priority mid-cycle. The discipline of betting only works if a loss (not being chosen) is treated as "not this cycle," not as a verdict on the idea's worth.

If a project doesn't finish by the end of its cycle, it ends by default — see [Building](building.md). Continuing it requires winning another bet, not an automatic extension. This is the mechanism that keeps unfinished work from silently accumulating.

**What would change this:** If the same pitch keeps losing at the table but keeps getting re-submitted, that's a sign the appetite or scope was wrong, not that the betting process needs to be bypassed — take it back to [Shaping](shaping.md) rather than forcing it through.
